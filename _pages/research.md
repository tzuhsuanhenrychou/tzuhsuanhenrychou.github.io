My overarching research goal is to enable **efficient and effective MIMO in future wireless systems**: acquiring high-dimensional channel knowledge with manageable training and signaling overhead, then converting that knowledge into performance gains through practical transceiver designs.

## Thrust 1 — Channel training and acquisition

**Wideband mmWave/sub-THz MIMO.** I developed compressed training methods for time-varying dual-wideband massive MIMO, accounting for beam squint and exploiting channel structure to reduce pilot overhead.

**Position-aided beam training.** I developed tensor-completion techniques that exploit spatial and beam-domain redundancy to recommend beams using sparse measurements.

**Cell-free MIMO localization.** Collaborative work explores efficient localization and fingerprint-based positioning across distributed antenna infrastructure.

### Future directions

- Scalable near-field and wideband channel acquisition for extremely large MIMO (XL-MIMO) arrays.
- Beam-focusing codebooks, joint near-/far-field channel models, and mobility-aware channel tracking.
- Synchronization-robust, low-overhead channel training and node placement in distributed MIMO, including UAV, satellite, and non-terrestrial networks.

## Thrust 2 — MIMO transceiver design

**Multi-UAV relay systems.** I studied joint UAV placement and distributed relay beamforming for multi-user networks, including location-dependent link-quality prediction when instantaneous channel information is unavailable before deployment.

**Probabilistic shaping.** My distribution-aware MIMO transceiver research incorporates nonuniform modulation symbol statistics into transceiver design to improve practical throughput.

**Hardware-awareness.** My ongoing work considers practical impairments and implementation constraints in MIMO systems.

### Future directions

- Hybrid near-/far-field XL-MIMO precoding and beam focusing in wideband multi-user scenarios.
- Robust, decentralized beamforming for distributed arrays under phase, Doppler, and channel uncertainty.
- Standards-compatible algorithms that translate signal-processing advances into practical wireless systems.

## Industry and standards interface

My work at Qualcomm includes physical-layer contributions to **IEEE 802.11** and **3GPP RAN1** in waveform, beam establishment, multi-antenna precoding, and transceiver design. I aim to bridge mathematical foundations, network-level performance, and realistic system constraints.
